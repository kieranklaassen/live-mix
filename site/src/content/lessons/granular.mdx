Cut a few hundredths of a second out of a sound, fade it in and out so that it has no edges, and what is left is a *grain*: too short to be a note or a word, long enough to keep the colour of what it was cut from. One grain alone is a blip. Granular sound is what happens when a great many are played, each started a little after the last: a new sound made of nothing but pieces of the old one.

Every grain has four things of its own, and they are the whole instrument: **where** in the sound it was cut from, **how long** it lasts, **how fast** it is read, and **when** it starts. Read every grain from the same place at the sound's own speed, each overlapping the one before by half, and the pieces add back up to what they were cut from. Read them faster and the pitch rises while the pace stays where it was: the pitch shifter (<Lesson id="pitch-shifting" />), with as many read heads as there are grains in place of two. Start them at random, from scattered places, and the sound smears into a cloud.

The engine's Grain Delay is a delay line (<Lesson id="delay-and-echo" />) that is never read straight. It writes what comes in onto a ring of memory, and everything it puts out is grains read back from that ring. These are its stages, in the signal's order. Press Play, then Listen on any stage that is sound.

## Stage by stage

### 1. A grain: a piece of the past under a window

A grain is a short read of the ring. It starts at a place <M t="p_0" />, steps on by its speed <M t="r" /> for each sample it puts out, and lasts <M t="L" /> samples. What it reads is multiplied by a window <M t="w" /> that opens from nothing and closes to nothing over the same <M t="L" /> samples:

<M block t={String.raw`g[n] = w\!\left(\frac{n}{L}\right)\, x\bigl(p_0 + r\,n\bigr), \qquad 0 \le n < L`} />

In the code this is the inside of <Src path="cpp/kit/grains.h" lines="53-72">`GrainPool::render`</Src>, three lines for each grain: add `source.read(channel, position) * window * gain` to the output, then `position += rate` and `phase += phase_step`, where the phase is <M t="n/L" /> and its step <M t="1/L" />. When the phase reaches 1 the grain is over and its place in the pool is free. The pool owns no sound. It asks whatever it is handed for `read(channel, position)`, and the Grain Delay hands it its ring (<Src path="cpp/devices/grain-delay/grain_delay.h" lines="175-199">`Tape`</Src>), which answers for a place between two samples with a four-point Hermite curve through its neighbours.

The lab picks one grain out of the device's pool as it starts and runs the same read again alone, in a pool of one.

### 2. The window, and why it is there

The kit's window has one number, its *shape* <M t="s" />, which sets how much of the grain is fade. With an edge <M t={String.raw`e = s/2`} /> and <M t={String.raw`\varphi = n/L`} /> the grain's phase:

<M block t={String.raw`w(\varphi) = \begin{cases} \tfrac12 - \tfrac12\cos\dfrac{\pi\varphi}{e} & \varphi < e \\[6pt] 1 & e \le \varphi \le 1 - e \\[4pt] \tfrac12 - \tfrac12\cos\dfrac{\pi(1-\varphi)}{e} & \varphi > 1 - e \end{cases}`} />

This is <Src path="cpp/kit/grains.h" lines="86-90">`window_at`</Src>. Each end is half a cycle of a cosine, lifted to run from 0 to 1, and the top is flat between them. At <M t="s = 1" /> the two fades meet in the middle and the window is the Hann bell, <M t={String.raw`\tfrac12 - \tfrac12\cos 2\pi\varphi`} />. That is the only shape the Grain Delay asks for. The kit will not go below a shape of 0.02, where the window is fully open for <Claim id="shape-least">98 % of the grain</Claim> and each fade is a hundredth of it.

<Controls ids="shape" />

  The window of the grain above. At Shape 1 it is the bell the device uses. Pull Shape down and the top flattens while the two fades grow steep.

The fades are not there for looks. A piece cut out with no window starts and stops wherever the wave happened to be, and a wave that jumps from nothing to something in one sample is a click: a step holds every frequency at once. A window that opens gently has no step to make.

### 3. When grains start: Size and Density

The pool does not decide when a grain starts. The device does, with a counter of samples (<Src path="cpp/devices/grain-delay/grain_delay.h" lines="257-294">`spawn_grain`</Src>): when it runs out a grain starts and the counter is wound up again by

<M block t={String.raw`\text{wait} = \max\!\Bigl(8,\; \frac{L}{D}\,(1 + j)\Bigr) \qquad\qquad R = \frac{D}{T_{\text{size}}}`} />

samples. <M t="L" /> is Size in samples and <M t="D" /> is Density. The <M t="j" /> is a random nudge that is zero for now; stage 6 is about it. Notice what Density is here. Textbooks often give the name to the number of grains a second and work out from it how many overlap, grains a second times the length of one. This device turns that round: its Density *is* the number of grains sounding at once, from 1 to 8, and the grains a second, <M t="R" />, follow from it. So no setting leaves a gap between grains. At 1 each grain ends on the sample the next begins.

With Size at 100 ms and Density at 4 every grain starts <Claim id="steady-wait">25 ms after the last</Claim>, to the sample.

### 4. What the grains add up to

The pool adds every sounding grain into one output. With no pitch change and no spray, every grain reads the same sample of the ring at the same instant, so the sum is that sample times the sum of the windows over it. The lab reads each grain's window and gain out of the pool and adds them up, which the device never does on its own account:

<Controls ids="density" />

<Trace taps="cover" from={1} span={1} min={0}>
  The windows of all sounding grains, each times its gain, added up over the same second as the cloud above. With Pitch at 0: at Density 1 it is a row of bells that touch the floor, from 2 a flat line at 1, and between whole numbers it ripples. Move Pitch and the grains no longer start in step, so the sum heaves.
</Trace>

Hann bells have a property that makes them the usual window for this work: spaced evenly, <M t="D" /> to a grain's length, they add up to a constant,

<M block t={String.raw`\sum_k w\!\left(\varphi - \frac{k}{D}\right) = \frac{D}{2}, \qquad D = 2, 3, 4, \dots`} />

so the sum of the grains is the recording itself, later. At Time 250 ms and any whole Density from 2 to 8 the device's grains match its own recording a quarter of a second earlier to <Claim id="plain-delay">under −89 dB</Claim>: a plain delay, built the long way round. It is not exact, because each grain counts its way through its window in single precision, one small step a sample, and the rounding of some thousands of additions leaves the windows' sum within 0.0001 of one and not on it. At Density 1 the bells only touch. The sum of the windows falls to nothing between grains and rises to <Claim id="bells-peak">1.63</Claim> in the middle of each, and the grains are the recording, Time late, times that row of bells to <Claim id="grain-in-device">within −120 dB</Claim>. That is what the red wave at the top of the page shows.

Why 1.63 and not 1? Because the device holds the *level* steady as Density moves, and it has to know which of two ways the grains add. Grains reading the same sound add in amplitude, and <M t="D" /> bells sum to <M t="D/2" />. Grains reading unrelated sound (other places, other speeds) add in power, like separate players, and <M t="D" /> bells carry a mean power of <M t="3D/8" />, since the mean square of one Hann bell is <M t="3/8" />. Each grain's gain is one over the root of whichever applies:

<M block t={String.raw`g = \frac{\sqrt2}{\sqrt{P}}, \qquad P = c\,P_{\text{same}} + (1-c)\,P_{\text{apart}}, \qquad P_{\text{same}} = \frac{D^2}{4}\bigl(1 + 2\rho^2\bigr), \quad P_{\text{apart}} = \frac{3D}{8}`} />

These are <Src path="cpp/devices/grain-delay/grain_delay.h" lines="228-255">`coherent_power`, `scattered_power` and `coherence`</Src>. The <M t={String.raw`\rho`} /> is the ripple of the windows' sum, one half at Density 1 and nothing at any whole Density from 2 up, which is where 1.63 comes from: <M t={String.raw`1/\sqrt{3/8}`} />. The <M t={String.raw`\sqrt2`} /> undoes the 3 dB that the pool's constant-power pan takes off a grain at the centre. And <M t="c" /> is the device's guess, between 0 and 1, at how alike overlapping grains are: 1 with Pitch at 0, no sprays and no Reverse, falling quickly as any of them moves. On white noise the grains' sum stays <Claim id="level-holds">within 0.2 dB of the recording's level</Claim> from Density 1 to 8, at Pitch 0 and at an octave up.

### 5. Where each grain reads, and how fast: Time, Spray and Pitch

Each grain is placed as it starts. The device throws its dice, then works out the place on the ring of the grain's first sample:

<M block t={String.raw`r = 2^{\,(\text{Pitch} \,+\, 12\,\text{PitchSpray}^2\, b)/12} \qquad d = \bigl(\text{Time} + \text{Spray}^2\, u\bigr)\, f_s \qquad p_0 = \text{now} - d - \tfrac12\,(r - 1)\,L`} />

Here <M t="u" /> is a random number from 0 to 1 and <M t="b" /> one from −1 to 1, new for each grain; with Spray in seconds, as its square is. The speed <M t="r" /> is the ratio of two frequencies that many semitones apart, held between an eighth and 8 (<Src path="cpp/kit/math.h">`semitones_to_ratio`</Src>). The delay <M t="d" /> is how old the sound is that the grain's *middle* reads. That is what the last term is for: a grain reading at twice the speed covers <M t="2L" /> samples of ring in <M t="L" /> samples of time, so it has to start half a grain further back to be passing the right place at its loudest. Both sprays are squared, so the first half of each knob is fine adjustment: Spray reaches up to a second later, Pitch Spray up to an octave either way.

### 6. Steady starts and scattered ones

A row of grains started at a steady rate is a pattern that repeats, and anything that repeats <M t="R" /> times a second has a pitch of its own at <M t="R" />. Put a sine of frequency <M t="f_0" /> through grains started every <M t={String.raw`T = 1/R`} /> seconds:

<M block t={String.raw`y(t) = \sum_k w(t - kT)\,\sin 2\pi f_0\bigl(kT + r\,(t - kT)\bigr)`} />

Move on by one wait, <M t={String.raw`t \to t + T`} />, and every term becomes its neighbour with the same extra turn of phase, <M t={String.raw`2\pi f_0 T`} />. A signal like that can only hold the frequencies <M t={String.raw`f_0 + mR`} />: lines <M t="R" /> hertz apart, counted from the *source's* frequency. The grain's own shape decides only how strong each line is, and it favours the ones near the shifted pitch <M t={String.raw`r f_0`} />. The lab plays a 1 kHz sine through a second copy of the device at the settings on this page, and through the kit's pool beside it, started by the device's formulas with the random nudge on a control of the lab's own.

With Pitch at 0, Size at 20 ms and Density at 1, there are 50 grains a second and the sine comes out as <Claim id="three-lines">three lines: itself, and one 50 Hz either side, 6 dB under it</Claim>. Those two are the row of bells, which is a cosine at 50 Hz, multiplying the sine. At Density 2 the bells sum to one and the side lines are gone: what is left beside the sine is <Claim id="no-lines">55 dB under it</Claim>.

Now shift the pitch. At +7 semitones the sine should land on 1498 Hz. With Size at 100 ms and Density at 4 there are 40 grains a second, and with steady starts the only frequencies allowed are 1000 + 40<M t="m" />: the output is <Claim id="comb-lines">two lines, at 1480 and 1520 Hz, 18 under the pitch and 22 over it</Claim>, and nothing on the pitch itself. Worse, the bell's own spectrum is nearly empty just there, for the same reason that overlapping bells add up to a constant, so the whole output is <Claim id="comb-level">23 dB down</Claim>. A steady grain rate has made a comb filter of the pitch shifter. Which notes pass depends on where they sit against the grain rate.

The cure is the nudge <M t="j" /> of stage 3. The device moves each wait by a random amount up to half of itself, in proportion to how unalike it reckons the grains are, <M t={String.raw`j = \tfrac12\,(1 - c)\,b`} />. With Pitch an octave up, Size at 100 ms and Density at 4, the waits run <Claim id="wait-range">from 12.5 to 37.5 ms</Claim> around their 25. The pattern no longer repeats, the lines dissolve, and the same sine lands on its pitch: <Claim id="scattered">all but a hundredth of its power lies within 20 Hz of 1498 Hz</Claim>. The cost is that a line has become a narrow band of noise, as wide as one grain's spectrum. This is why the device has no control for it. With grains that agree, a steady rate is what makes them sum to a plain delay, and with grains that do not, a steady rate is a fault. Hear the two with the saw and Pitch at 7: <Listen tap="steady">the lab's pool, steady</Listen> against <Listen tap="grains">the device's grains</Listen>.

### 7. Round again: feedback and tone

After the pool the device does four small things (<Src path="cpp/devices/grain-delay/grain_delay.h" lines="124-144">the end of its sample loop</Src>). It limits the sum with a curve that is exactly linear up to full scale, so only a pile-up of grains in step is held. It low-passes it at Tone (<Src path="cpp/kit/filters.h">the kit's `Svf`</Src> at <M t="Q = 0.6" />), high-passes it at 30 Hz, and sends the result two ways: to the output, and back to be recorded with the input,

<M block t={String.raw`\text{recorded} = \operatorname{clip}\bigl(\operatorname{guard}(\text{in} + \text{Feedback}\cdot\text{wet})\bigr) \qquad \text{out} = \cos\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}m\bigr)\,\text{in} + \sin\!\bigl(\tfrac{\pi}{2}m\bigr)\,\text{wet}`} />

with <M t="m" /> the Mix. The grains read what the grains made. Feedback in a plain delay gives the same sound again, quieter; here each trip round the loop goes through the pool again, so each repeat is shifted again by Pitch, scattered again by the sprays and darkened again by Tone. With Pitch at 0 and Feedback at 0.5 each repeat comes back <Claim id="repeat-level">6.1 dB under the last</Claim>. With Pitch at +12 a 220 Hz note returns <Claim id="octave-stair">at 440, then 880, then 1760 Hz</Claim>, a stair of octaves half a second apart at a Time of 500 ms.

## What goes wrong

**Aliasing.** A grain read at twice the speed doubles every frequency in it, and anything above a quarter of the sample rate would land above half of it and fold back down (<Lesson id="samples-and-aliasing" />). The device does not filter each grain. It low-passes what it *records*, with <Src path="cpp/devices/grain-delay/grain_delay.h" lines="296-311">a filter</Src> that follows the fastest upward speed in use, at <M t={String.raw`0.45 f_s / r`} />.

<Controls ids="pitch" />

<Spectrum taps="in recorded" min={-100} max={-10}>
  The spectrum of the loop (grey) and of what the device records (red). With the saw, raise Pitch: the red harmonics fall away above a corner that drops an octave for each octave of shift. At Pitch 0 or below, only red shows.
</Spectrum>

At +12 semitones the recording is <Claim id="guard">3 dB down at 10.8 kHz and 10 dB down at 15 kHz</Claim>; at 0 or below nothing is taken. The price is that an upward shift is always a little dull, and that Pitch Spray dulls it further, since the filter must allow for the fastest grain the dice could throw.

**The level between the two cases.** The guess <M t="c" /> of stage 4 was fitted to band-limited noise, and it takes sound to stay like itself over a fifth of a millisecond. Bright sound does not. With white noise, Density at 4 and Pitch only a fiftieth of a semitone off zero, the device still treats the grains as agreeing and the output drops <Claim id="level-model">5.4 dB</Claim>. On the guitar you will not find it; on noise you can.

**Reading what has not been written.** A grain that reads faster than time gains on the write point, and one long enough would overtake it. The device will not place a grain where that could happen, which puts a floor under Time of <M t={String.raw`|r - 1|\,L/2`} /> forwards and <M t={String.raw`(1 + r)\,L/2`} /> reversed, plus four samples for the Hermite curve. With Time at 10 ms, Size at 500 ms and Pitch at +12 the grains start <Claim id="time-floor">500.08 ms behind</Claim> and end four samples from the write point: the delay you get is a quarter of a second, not the 10 ms you asked for.

**Running out of grains.** The pool holds 20 and a grain that finds it full is dropped without a word. The device stays clear of that: at Density 8 with every wait scattered, the most that sounded at once in the loop was <Claim id="most-grains">11 of the 20</Claim>.

**Old sound after a silence.** After enough silence the device goes to sleep, and <Src path="cpp/devices/grain-delay/grain_delay.h" lines="106-115">when it wakes</Src> it remembers where on the ring it woke. A grain that reaches back past that point reads silence, so the first note after a pause never brings back a piece of the last one.

## What to try

- Guitar, Density 1, and sweep **Size** from 500 ms down to 10 ms: phrases, then notes, then a buzz at the grain rate with the guitar's colour.
- **Density** 1, then 2: pulses, then the plain delay. Stop between them, near 1.5, and listen to the ripple.
- **Pitch** +12 with Density at 4. Then raise **Feedback** to 0.5 and watch the stair in the last figure.
- **Spray** from 0 to 1 with the drums: the hits stop being hits. Add **Reverse** at 0.5.
- The saw, **Pitch** 7, Size 100 ms, Density 4, and the two Listen buttons of stage 6: the steady comb against the scattered grains.
- The chords, Size 400 ms, Density 8, Spray 0.7, Pitch Spray 0.3, Feedback 0.9, Tone 3 kHz: a note held long after it was played.
