<Part id="why" title="A chord dies in two seconds. Suppose you want it to stay.">

A guitar chord is strummed and left to ring. Two seconds later it has all but gone, and the next chord is struck.

<Still
  taps="in"
  caption="The loop against time: two chords, each struck and left to die. Notice how soon each chord has fallen to a fraction of its first height."
/>

Suppose you want the first chord to stay: not the strum, only the sound of the chord a moment after it, held under everything that follows for as long as you like. A recording has that moment once, and it lasts a few hundredths of a second.

The first thing anyone tries is to cut that moment out and play it round and round. Here it is: <Src path="cpp/devices/spectral-blur/spectral_blur.h" at="static constexpr int kFrame = 2048;">2,048 samples</Src> of the chord, which is <Claim id="slice">42.7 ms</Claim>, repeated end to end. <Hear set="loop" label="Hear the slice repeated" />

<Still
  taps="looped"
  set="loop"
  from={0.34}
  span={0.1}
  caption="A tenth of a second of the repeated slice. The slice ends at one height and begins at another, so there is a step where it joins itself. Notice the step come round again and again, once for every turn of the slice."
/>

It is not the chord. The step where the slice joins itself is a click, and the click comes round at a steady rate, which the ear hears as a buzz with a pitch of its own. Worse, a sound that repeats exactly can only hold frequencies that fit its length a whole number of times. Put a plain 440 Hz tone through the same loop, and what comes out is not at 440 Hz at all: it is <Claim id="loop-lines">a row of lines 23.4 Hz apart, the strongest at 445.3 Hz, with the lines either side of it 10.7 dB and 14.6 dB under it</Claim>.

<Still
  taps="linesIn:in linesLooped:out"
  set="loop"
  source="sine"
  view="spectrum"
  scale="linear"
  top={1000}
  min={-80}
  max={0}
  caption="A 440 Hz tone, and the same tone as a repeated slice, up to 1 kHz. Notice that the one peak has become a comb, and that no tooth of the comb stands at 440 Hz."
/>

<Lesson id="short-time-fourier" cap /> offers something better than a slice of the wave. It cuts the sound into overlapping frames, turns each into bins, and adds the frames back so smoothly that nothing can be heard where they join. A frame's bins say how much of each frequency the moment holds, which is exactly what you want to keep. So keep them: take one frame's bins and lay that same frame down again at every hop, for ever.

<Predict
  question="One frame's bins are kept just as they are, and that frame is laid down again every 512 samples, overlapping its neighbours as frames do. A steady 440 Hz tone goes in. What comes out?"
  choices={['The tone, held', 'A steady tone at another pitch', 'A buzz with no pitch']}
>

A steady tone at another pitch. The joins are smooth now, so there is no click. But one frame laid down again at every hop makes a sound that comes round at every hop, and <Claim id="stuck-pitch">a sound that repeats every 512 samples can only hold multiples of 93.75 Hz. The tone moves to the nearest one, 468.75 Hz, a semitone sharp, and comes out 5.4 dB quieter</Claim>. <Hear set="stuck" label="Hear the tone come out sharp" />

<Still
  taps="linesIn:in linesStuck:out"
  set="stuck"
  view="spectrum"
  scale="linear"
  top={1000}
  min={-80}
  max={0}
  caption="The 440 Hz tone, and one frame of it laid down again and again, up to 1 kHz. Notice that the peak has moved up, to a place where a sound that repeats every hop can have one."
/>

The frame was not wrong. What was wrong was treating it as a still picture. Every bin of a frame is a size and an angle, and in a sound that is going on, the angle of every bin turns from one frame to the next, each by its own amount. Hold the angles still and you have asked every frequency to come round exactly once a hop, which only those few can do.

</Predict>

So the sizes may be kept, and the angles must be kept turning. That is the whole idea of this lesson, and it works: the picture this page opens on is the chord held that way by Spectral Blur, a device of the engine. Three tenths of a second into the loop its Freeze is switched on, and from there the chord neither fades nor buzzes. <Hear set="held" label="Hear the chord held" />

Holding is only the first thing that can be done once a sound lies as bins. The sizes can be let fall slowly, so that a sound hangs behind itself. The angles can be scrambled, which smears every attack across its frame and leaves the colour. Each bin can be given a gain of its own, or copied an octave up, or sorted by how it behaves and sent one way or another. All of it happens in the gap between the transform and its inverse, and the rest of the page goes through that gap one piece at a time.

</Part>

Check one thing against it. As the page opens, Freeze goes on while the first chord rings, and the picture is taken two seconds later, just after the second chord is struck. So the two rows are two different chords: the tallest bar coming in stands at <Claim id="held-bars">258 Hz, and the tallest held bar at 328 Hz</Claim>. The held row is the first chord, and <Claim id="held-same">every bar of it is exactly the size its bin had in the frame Freeze caught</Claim>, though the sound that made it died away long ago.

That is all that holding is. A frame is a row of sizes, and a device that keeps the row instead of taking the next one has stopped the sound in its tracks. The three controls are three ways of deciding what a bin holds. "Frame at" picks the moment. Freeze keeps every bar for good. Blur lets each bar follow its input up at once and come down slowly, so that the row always shows a little of what was there before.

Two more pictures belong beside this one, and both come back in part 5. The first is the same row of bars in Sift, a second device. Sift holds nothing. It gives every bin a share between none and all, and passes on that much of it: a gate with a setting for each bin.

<Part id="build" title="Seven pieces: split, hold, turn, smear, fall, an octave, a gain.">

Spectral Blur's frame is put together here a piece at a time, in the lab's own code. The frames themselves are those of <Lesson id="short-time-fourier" />, at the sizes the device uses: <Src path="cpp/devices/spectral-blur/spectral_blur.h" at="static constexpr int kFrame = 2048;">2,048 samples</Src>, a new one every <Src path="cpp/devices/spectral-blur/spectral_blur.h" at="static constexpr int kHop = 512;">512</Src>. Each step adds one small function between the transform and its inverse. The sound of every step is real, so press "Hear it so far" as you go. Step 2 sounds wrong, and it is meant to: it is the experiment of part 1, and step 3 is the cure.

"Frame at" and Freeze work as they did in part 2. The frame that "Frame at" picks is the one Freeze catches, and the pictures of bars are taken two seconds after it.

<Build>
  <Step title="Sizes and angles" controls="at">
    Begin by doing nothing, with care. A frame goes through the transform and comes out as bins, each a pair of numbers. Read as a point on a plane, a bin is an arrow from the middle: it has a length, and it points somewhere. The length is how much of that frequency the frame holds, and this page calls it the bin's size. The direction is the bin's angle: where in its swing that frequency stood when the frame began. This step takes every bin apart into the two, puts them together again, and sends the frame back.

    <Listing
      path="site/labs/spectral-processing.cpp"
      from="// Analyse: the newest 2,048 samples times the window, as 1,025 bins."
      to="unit_im[k] = level[k] > 1.0e-12f ? in_im[k] * inverse : 0.0f;"
      mark={['level[k] = std::sqrt(in_re[k] * in_re[k] + in_im[k] * in_im[k]);', 'unit_re[k] = level[k] > 1.0e-12f ? in_re[k] * inverse : 1.0f;']}
      name="split"
    />

    The size is the length of the arrow, by Pythagoras. The angle is kept as an arrow too: the bin divided by its own length, which points the same way and is one unit long. No angle in degrees or radians is ever worked out, here or in the device. Turning an arrow by an angle is multiplying it by another arrow of length one, and that is all the later pieces need.

    <Listing
      path="site/labs/spectral-processing.cpp"
      from="// Join: size times angle is a bin again."
      to="for (int n = 0; n < kFrame; ++n) sum[(out_start + n) & kMask] += re[n] * hann[n] * (1.0f / (1.5f * kFrame));"
      mark={['re[k] = out[k] * angle_re[k];', 'im[k] = out[k] * angle_im[k];']}
      name="join"
    />

    `join` reads two things this step has not touched: `size`, which is what the bin holds, and `angle_re` and `angle_im`, the angle it goes out at. For now they are the bin's own size and its own angle, so the bins that go back are the bins that came in, and `rebuild` adds the frames up as the earlier lesson showed. What comes out is the sound that went in, <Claim id="built-wire">2,304 samples late, with the difference between the two more than 130 dB under the sound</Claim>. Every later step works by making `size` or the angle differ from what came in.

  <Step title="Hold a frame" controls="at freeze">
    Now hold the bars. `hold` decides, for every bin of every frame, what the bin holds from here on. A bin is driven while it takes its size from the input. With Freeze on no bin is driven, and each keeps the size it had.

    <Listing
      path="site/labs/spectral-processing.cpp"
      from="// What each bin holds. A bin is driven while the input reaches what it"
      to="size[k] = kept;"
      mark="driven[k] = !frozen && level[k] >= kept;"
      name="hold"
    />

    The function is written with step 5 in mind. Until then `fall` is 0 and `rise` is 1, so with Freeze off `kept` starts at nothing, every bin is driven, and `kept` ends as the size that came in: step 1 again. With Freeze on, `fall` is 1 and nothing is driven, so `kept` is last frame's size, untouched. The line under the test drops a bin that holds less than <Src path="cpp/devices/spectral-blur/spectral_blur.h" at="static constexpr float kMagnitudeFloor = 1.0e-6f;">a millionth</Src>, far under anything that can be heard, so that a held frame can be told from silence.

    A bin that is not driven has no input to take its angle from. At this step it goes out at the angle it went out at last time. So with Freeze on, the same frame goes back at every hop, sizes and angles alike: the experiment of part 1, in the build. From the catch on, the output <Claim id="hold-repeats">repeats every 512 samples, with the difference from one hop to the next more than 120 dB under the sound</Claim>. It is also the lab's own "one frame laid down again and again" of part 1, which was made with no transform at all: the two differ by <Claim id="stuck-built">an amount more than 130 dB under the sound</Claim>.

    Hear it so far. The chord is held, and it buzzes at the rate of the hop. On the sine in the row of sounds it is a clean tone at the wrong pitch.

  <Step title="Keep the bins turning" controls="at freeze">
    What step 2 lacks is in the angles. While a partial sounds, each of its bins turns by the same angle from one frame to the next, and how far it turns is set by the partial's frequency: <Lesson id="short-time-fourier" /> worked that out in its stage on the phase between frames. A held bin whose angle stands still is claiming a frequency that comes round exactly once a hop. So measure each bin's turn while it still has an input, and keep the bin turning by that much after the input has gone.

    <Listing
      path="site/labs/spectral-processing.cpp"
      from="// The turn: this frame's bin times the conjugate of the last one's is a"
      to="last_im[k] = in_im[k];"
      mark={['const float tr = xr * lr + xi * li;', 'const float ti = xi * lr - xr * li;', 'const float turned = angle_re[k] * tr - angle_im[k] * ti;']}
      name="turning"
    />

    `measure_turn` multiplies this frame's bin by last frame's bin laid over on its back, which is its conjugate. The product is an arrow whose angle is the difference of the two angles: the turn. Its length is of no use and is taken out when the turn is used. The test above it measures only while the bin's input is holding up, because a bin whose input is being cut off turns by the cut and not by the sound.

    `angles` then gives each bin the angle it goes out at. A driven bin goes out at the angle it came in at. A held bin goes out at its last angle times its turn, which is the two angles added. The line with `keep` nudges the arrow back to a length of one, since thousands of multiplications would otherwise let it creep.

    With this piece the held sine is at its own pitch. Freeze holds the 440 Hz sine at <Claim id="turn-440">440.0 Hz where step 2 gave 468.75 Hz, and what is held is the tone that went in, carried on: the two differ by an amount more than 75 dB under the tone</Claim>. Hear it so far on the chords: the buzz has gone.

  <Step title="Smear the angles" controls="at freeze smear">
    A frame that only turns is a fixed handful of sines, and nothing in it will ever move. It also keeps every angle of the moment it was caught, and with them the exact shape of whatever was happening then. The fourth piece loosens the angles: every frame, each bin's angle is turned by a random amount, and Smear says how far, up to half a turn either way at its top.

    <Listing
      path="site/labs/spectral-processing.cpp"
      from="// Smear: every bin's angle is turned by a random amount, up to half a turn"
      to="angle_re[k] = rotated;"
      mark="const float steps = angle * (driven[k] ? driven_steps : held_steps);"
      name="scatter"
    />

    The same lines do two different things. A driven bin starts again from the input's angle on every frame, so its random turns never add up: it is only ever a little way from where the input put it. A held bin has nothing to start again from, so its turns pile up and its angle wanders further and further from where it began. And while Freeze is on, the held bins are smeared as if Smear were at least <Src path="cpp/devices/spectral-blur/spectral_blur.h" at="static constexpr float kFreezeDrift = 0.12f;">0.12</Src>, wherever the control stands. A frozen sound in this device always moves a little.

    How much it moves depends on how many partials share the work. One sine lies in three bins that now wander apart from one another, so they add up differently from moment to moment: with Freeze on and Smear at 0, <Claim id="drift-span">the level of the held 440 Hz sine, read every 50 ms from 1.6 s on, ranges over 9 dB, where step 3 held it within 0.01 dB</Claim>. A chord has dozens of partials to average over, and the drone of part 3 is the result.

    Random angles also cost level where four frames overlap, and `join` has two gains to pay it back, one for driven bins and one for held. Stage 5 of part 5 works them out.

  <Step title="Let the sizes fall" controls="at freeze smear blur">
    Freeze holds for ever. Blur holds for a while. Each frame, a bin keeps what it held times a number a little under 1, and takes its size from the input only when the input reaches that.

    <Listing
      path="site/labs/spectral-processing.cpp"
      from="const float seconds = Blur::kMaxDecaySeconds * s.blur * s.blur * s.blur;"
      to="hold(frozen, frozen ? 1.0f : fall, rise);"
      mark={['fall = seconds > 0.0f ? std::exp(-6.9077553f * kHop / (seconds * lab::kSampleRate)) : 0.0f;', 'rise = 1.0f - Blur::kAttackSoftening * s.blur;']}
      name="falling"
    />

    `seconds` is how long a held bin takes to fall 60 dB: <Src path="cpp/devices/spectral-blur/spectral_blur.h" at="static constexpr float kMaxDecaySeconds = 20.0f;">20 seconds</Src> times Blur cubed, so that most of the control's travel is spent on short hangs. `fall` is the factor for one hop that gets there. Read `hold` in step 2 again with `fall` under 1. `kept` is what the bin held, a little less. If the input reaches that, the bin is driven and follows the input. If not, the bin is held and keeps `kept`. A loud note takes its bins over at once, and when it stops they come down at Blur's pace, each at the frequency step 3 gave it.

    `rise` softens the way up. At Blur 0 a driven bin takes the input's size at once. Higher up it covers only part of the distance each frame, <Src path="cpp/devices/spectral-blur/spectral_blur.h" at="static constexpr float kAttackSoftening = 0.7f;">0.7 times Blur</Src> short of all of it, so that a bin does not ride every chance peak of a noisy input.

    The lab's test puts in a 440 Hz tone that fades out over a twentieth of a second, with Freeze off and Blur at 0.5. From then on the bin the tone was in <Claim id="fall-24">falls 24 dB a second; with this piece switched off it is empty by the next frame</Claim>.

<Part id="wrong" title="Five faults, and what to do about each.">

<Faults>
  <Fault title="What is caught depends on the moment" set="caughtHit">
    <Still
      slot="picture"
      taps="in out"
      set="caughtHit"
      caption="The drums and what Freeze holds when it catches a hit. Notice that the held sound keeps the height the hit had in the one frame that was caught, however short the hit was."
    />

    Freeze keeps one frame, and a frame is a few hundredths of a second of whatever was going on. On a held chord any frame will do. On a sound that moves, a hair's difference changes everything. <Claim id="catch-moment">Caught 0.5 s into the drum loop, on a hit, the frozen sound stands at −19 dB. Caught 0.55 s in, it stands 25 dB lower, and caught at 0.4 s, between two hits, there is nothing to hold</Claim>. A frozen hit is also no longer a hit: it is the hit's colour, held as a steady noise. What to do: catch a sound while it rings, not while it strikes. In a track, automate Freeze to go on after the attack, or put the device on a send and catch only what is sent. For a note that has to be held cleanly, Sustain does the catching itself and waits for the attack to pass.

  </Fault>
  <Fault title="What is played next retunes what is held" set="retuned">
    Freeze holds the size of every bin, and stage 4 of part 5 showed that it goes on taking each bin's turn from the input. So whatever is played into the device after the catch, in a bin that is held and at more than a tenth of the held size, moves that held partial to its own pitch. This card plays the chords as the page opens them: the first chord is caught, and the second is struck over it. Listen at the two-second mark for the held chord to shift: its loudest bin moves a semitone, and the chord that was caught is no longer quite the chord that sounds. What to do: once the sound is caught, stop feeding the device. On a send, close the send after the catch, and nothing arrives to retune the held bins. On an insert with Mix at a half, expect the held sound to lean towards whatever is played over it. Sustain has no such fault: it measures its turns once, at the catch.
  </Fault>
  <Fault title="Sound ahead of the attack" set="scramble">
    A bin's angle is what puts an attack at its place inside the frame. Turn the angles at random and the attack spreads over the whole frame, and half of a frame lies before the moment it should have come: stage 5 of part 5 measured half of a single sample's energy arriving early. This card plays the drums with Smear at its top and nothing else. Listen for a breath before each hit and for the hits themselves gone soft. What to do: keep Smear low on anything that strikes, or lower Mix so that the plain sound carries the attacks and the smeared sound only follows them. Sift offers a third way, which is to take the attacks out first and smear only what is left.
  </Fault>
  <Fault title="Louder than it went in" set="loud">
    Mix weighs the plain sound against the frames by a law made for two sounds that have nothing to do with each other. With Blur and Smear down and Freeze off, the frames are the plain sound, the two add up in step, and the law overshoots: on this card, with Mix at a half, <Claim id="loud">the chords come out 3.0 dB over the level they went in at</Claim>. What to do: nothing, once the device is doing its work, since a held or smeared sound is no longer in step with the plain one. But a Mix at a half is not the place to check whether the device is switched in by comparing levels, and a setting that barely blurs will read a little hot.
  </Fault>
  <Fault title="It is always late">
    Nothing that works on frames answers sooner than a frame, and stages 8 and 10 of part 5 gave the two delays. Played by hand through Spectral Blur, a part is heard a frame and half a hop after the key went down, and through Sift later still, because Sift waits for the frames that follow before it sorts one. No setting shortens either. What to do: use these devices on tracks that are already recorded, where a host moves every other track to meet the delay. The engine's part is to be exact about it: one fixed number for each device, stated in its manifest, with the plain sound inside the device delayed to match.
  </Fault>
</Faults>

<Part id="myths" title="Six things that are often said, and what is true.">

<Myths>
  <Myth says="Freezing a sound is looping a short piece of it.">
    A looped piece can only hold frequencies that fit its length, so it turns a tone into a comb of other tones and adds a click where it joins itself: part 1 shows both. Freeze keeps no piece of the wave. It keeps a size and a turn for each bin, and step 3 of the build shows a held tone coming out at its own pitch.
  </Myth>
  <Myth says="The sizes of the bins are the sound, and the angles do not matter.">
    Step 2 of the build holds the sizes exactly and gets the angles wrong, and a tone comes out a semitone sharp. Stage 5 of part 5 keeps the sizes and scrambles the angles, and a single sample becomes a burst longer than a frame. The sizes say which frequencies. The angles say where in time, and how the frames join.
  </Myth>
  <Myth says="A spectral blur is a kind of reverb.">
    A reverb is made of echoes: copies of the sound that arrive later. Nothing here arrives later. Each bin simply stays where it was and comes down along the straight slope of stage 2, so a tail holds only the frequencies that were played and no reflections of anything. The hang card of part 3 is the sound of it.
  </Myth>
  <Myth says="A frozen sound is a recording of that moment.">
    It is one row of sizes and one turn for each bin, and the wave of the moment cannot be had back from them. Stage 3 shows the row held bar for bar. Stage 5 shows that the angles are then left to wander, so the frozen sound is never twice the same. Part 7 shows how much the result depends on which frame was caught, and that later playing can move its pitch.
  </Myth>
  <Myth says="Scrambling the angles throws the sound's energy away.">
    It would, by up to three quarters, if nothing were done about it, because four overlapping frames that disagree add up to less than four that agree. Stage 5 gives the gain the device applies to make that good, and the measurements beside it: the level of the chords holds at any Smear, and the energy of the single sample is all still there. What is lost is the place of things in time.
  </Myth>
  <Myth says="A bin is a frequency.">
    A bin is a place where nearby frequencies show up. Stage 1 shows one sine filling three of them, and stage 4 shows that the frequency itself is in how fast the bin turns: a tone between two bins is held at its own pitch and not at the centre of either. A device that treated bins as frequencies would hold every sound a little out of tune, which is step 2 of the build again.
  </Myth>
</Myths>
