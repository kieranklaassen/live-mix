# Beats and pulses: the techniques, and what is known

**The beat and breath family is nine devices**: six instruments (Binaural,
Isochronic, Coloured Noise, Breath Pacer, Still Point, Twin Bars) and three
effects (Ear Shift, Band Pulse, Winding Down). Each is a musical instrument or
effect first, named for its technique and described by what is heard. This
page says what each technique is, what research has measured, and where a
two-ear beat survives in a mix. Nothing here is a treatment, and nothing here
promises a listener anything.

Their controls and presets are listed in
[devices-generated.md](../devices-generated.md), what each costs and what its
checker found weak in [devices.md](../devices.md), and the laws they share in
the [family recipe](../recipes/beat-and-breath-family.md).

## What each technique is

### A beat between the ears

Two steady tones a few hertz apart, one to each ear. Neither ear receives any
pulsing: the waver is made inside the hearing system, where the two ears'
signals meet [[1]](#sources). It needs headphones, tones under about 1 kHz
(clearest near 400 to 500 Hz) and rates to about 30 Hz, and it is about 3 dB
deep. Some listeners hear it weakly or not at all [[2]](#sources).

Binaural plays each key as a pair Beat (0.5 to 40 Hz) apart, half of Beat
above and below the key, with the key folded by octaves into 80 Hz to 1 kHz.
Twin Bars strikes two bars Beat (0.5 to 20 Hz) apart on every partial. Ear
Shift moves the band between Low (60 to 400 Hz, 60 as it starts) and High
(400 to 2,000 Hz, 1,000 as it starts) up in the left ear and down in the
right, by half of Beat (0.5 to 40 Hz) each; a note within an octave of either
edge is part shifted and wavers in each ear at half of Beat.

### A beat in the air

The same two tones in both ears. They add to one tone whose loudness swings at
the difference, down to silence. It works on speakers and at any pitch.
Binaural, Twin Bars and Ear Shift give it at Ears 0; Binaural's Cross Layout
gives it in each ear at any Ears, two tones Beat apart in each, while the two
ears differ by half of Beat.

### A pulsed tone

One tone switched on and off evenly. Isochronic pulses each key, a tone or
with Air a band of noise at the key, at Rate (0.5 to 40 Hz): on for Duty (0.1
to 0.9) of each period, with edges of Edge (2 to 50 ms), falling by Depth, to
silence at 1. Keys under 80 Hz sound an octave or more up. It works on
speakers.

|                | Between the ears                  | In the air                      | Pulsed tone                                    |
| -------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Pulsing arises | In the hearing system             | In the air                      | In the air                                     |
| Depth          | About 3 dB                        | Up to full                      | Up to full                                     |
| Headphones     | Needed                            | Not needed                      | Not needed                                     |
| Limits         | Under about 1 kHz, to about 30 Hz | Any pitch                       | Any pitch                                      |
| Devices        | Binaural, Twin Bars, Ear Shift    | The same at Ears 0; Still Point | Isochronic; Band Pulse and Winding Down, below |

After [[3]](#sources) and [[1]](#sources).

### Pulsing a band of music

Band Pulse pulses only the band between Low (60 to 400 Hz) and High (400 to
4,000 Hz) of the sound fed to it, at Rate (0.5 to 40 Hz), with the same Duty,
Edge and Depth. Sound an octave outside the band keeps its level; nearer the
band it is pulsed in part. The gap is only as deep as the band is wide: at
Depth 1 the middle of a band four octaves wide falls 40 dB or more, and the
middle of the band as it starts (200 Hz to 1 kHz) falls 22.5 dB. At Depth 0 every
frequency leaves at the level it came in, turned in phase, which can lift a
peak. It needs no headphones.

### Noise colours

White noise is level in every hertz, pink falls 3 dB an octave and brown 6 dB.
A held key of Coloured Noise opens one bed: Colour runs from white (0) through
pink (0.5) to brown (1) at one level, Low cut and High cut trim it, Tint adds
a soft ringing band at each held key, and Drift is a slow, unpaced swell, each
one from half of Drift time (2 to 30 s) to one and a half times it. Nothing
beats.

### Paced breathing

The sound sets a pace and the listener breathes with it. Breath Pacer shapes a
swell of air and tone by In, Hold, Out and Rest in seconds, with a soft mark
at the key at the start of In and one a fifth below at the start of Out. In 5
and Out 5 is six breaths a minute. Slow to and Over lengthen the cycle, to at
most twice over at most 30 minutes.

### Two musical ideas

**A sine that sweeps through held tones.** Still Point sweeps one sine back
and forth from Reach (0.1 to 12 semitones) below the lowest held key to Reach
above the highest, a pass in Time (4 to 120 s). The beat against each key
slows to stillness as they meet and speeds away after. It is a beat in the
air.

**A pulse that seems to slow without end.** Winding Down lays 2 to 5 Layers of
one pulse, each twice as fast as the next, over the sound fed to it, and only
turns it down. They halve together every Halving (4 to 120 s) and fade in and
out around Rate (1 to 8 Hz), so the pulse seems always to slow, or with
Direction to quicken.

## What research supports and what it does not

This section reports what studies measured, in the words of that research.
None of it is a promise about a listener.

**Firm**

- **The hearing system follows real pulsing.** Loudness that pulses at a
  steady rate draws a small response at that rate in a recording at the scalp,
  largest near 40 Hz [[4]](#sources) [[5]](#sources). It shows the hearing pathways
  following the sound, and nothing beyond them.
- **A two-ear beat is real but weak**: about 3 dB of pulsing, on headphones
  only, tones under about 1 kHz, rates to about 30 Hz [[1]](#sources). Its measured
  response is smaller than that of a beat in the air [[6]](#sources).
- **Paced breathing near six breaths a minute raises heart-rate variability**,
  during a session and after weeks of practice, across 223 studies
  [[7]](#sources).
- **Slow, quiet, simple music.** Reviews of trials find that people fall
  asleep sooner and rate their sleep better; objective measures are
  inconsistent [[8]](#sources) [[9]](#sources).
- **Steady noise masks other sound.** Pink noise at 45 dB reduced the broken
  sleep that traffic noise caused in 12 young adults; alone it did not differ
  from quiet [[10]](#sources).

**Unsettled or not supported**

- **That a two-ear beat pulls the brain's rhythms to its rate.** Of 14 studies
  that recorded brain activity, 5 found it, 8 did not and 1 was mixed; none
  that set the beat in pink noise found it [[11]](#sources). A pooling of 22 small
  studies found an effect on memory, attention, anxiety and pain scores
  [[12]](#sources); the largest study, 920 adults at home, measured worse reasoning
  and concentration [[13]](#sources).
- **That a named band gives a named state.** Delta (0.5 to 4 Hz), theta (4 to
  8), alpha (8 to 13), beta (13 to 30) and gamma (30 to 100) name rhythms
  recorded in deep sleep, drowsiness, rest, engaged waking and sensory
  processing [[14]](#sources). That records what goes with what. It does not follow
  that playing a rate brings the state.
- **The 40 Hz work is early.** Mouse studies reported less amyloid after 40 Hz
  light or sound [[15]](#sources) [[16]](#sources); a larger repeat with light did
  not [[17]](#sources). In people it is under study as light with sound in
  Alzheimer's disease; one pilot followed five people without a randomised
  control [[18]](#sources).
- **Steady coloured noise for sleep.** A 2021 review rated the evidence very
  low quality [[19]](#sources), and in 25 adults continuous pink noise at 40 or
  50 dBA reduced REM sleep [[20]](#sources).
- **A pulsed tone has few studies of its own.** One trial of 37 adults
  measured no significant alpha change [[21]](#sources).

## Where the two-ear effect survives in a mix

A two-ear beat lasts only while each ear receives its own signal. Whatever
adds the two sides turns it into a beat in the air: a mono fold; speakers,
where the air mixes the sides; a pan off centre; a reverb, or any effect fed
from both sides, that sums them; a width control turned down.

**The Ears law.** With Ears `e`, each ear takes `(1 + e) / 2` of its own tone
and `(1 - e) / 2` of the other's. The left ear carries the higher tone, on
every device. The mid is the same at every setting, so a mono fold is a full
beat at Beat wherever Ears stands.

| Ears | Side over mid | Ripple inside each ear |
| ---- | ------------- | ---------------------- |
| 0    | none          | full depth             |
| 0.5  | -6.0 dB       | 6.0 dB                 |
| 0.8  | -1.9 dB       | 1.9 dB                 |
| 1    | 0 dB          | none                   |

At Ears 0 the two sides are identical: the beat is real pulsing in the air and
works on speakers.

- **Binaural** and **Twin Bars**: at 1 one tone, or bar, in each ear; between,
  each ear also ripples; at 0 both in the middle. Binaural starts at Ears 1,
  Twin Bars at 0.6. Binaural's bed of noise is not narrowed by Ears.
- **Ear Shift**: at 1 the band is up in the left ear and down in the right,
  and its mono fold pulses to silence at Beat; at 0 both ears carry one pulse
  at Beat. It starts at 1.
- **Still Point**: at 0, where it starts, sine and keys share both ears; at 1
  the keys are in the left ear, the sine in the right, and neither ear dips
  where they meet.
- **Coloured Noise**, Width: the same law on two unlike noises, from one noise
  on both sides (0) to its own on each (1). A mono fold is as loud at every
  Width.
- **Offset** (Isochronic, Band Pulse, Winding Down): the right ear's pulse
  runs up to 180 degrees behind the left's. At 0 the ears pulse together;
  between, the right ear's pulse comes after the left's; at 180 degrees each
  ear fills the other's gap, and the pulse is gone in mono when the sound is
  on for half of each period: Duty 0.5 on Isochronic and Band Pulse, always
  on Winding Down, whose layers are half on and half off.

Bank presets keep Ears and Width at 0.8 or under, which holds their side
under their mid as the bank's width rule asks, and Offset at 120 degrees or
under, so a mono fold never loses the pulse. A device's own presets go to the
ends.

### What the engine has measured

Nobody has listened to any of this. The figures below are of the built
devices at 48 kHz, each as it starts unless a setting is named. A tone was fed
to each input, 6 Hz apart, the pair a key makes at Beat 6 and Ears 1, on six
keys from 110 to 660 Hz, and read over four seconds once any tail had filled.
Each figure is how far the other ear's tone stands under the ear's own at the
worse ear, over the six keys.

| After the pair               | The other ear's tone                       |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| `ambient-eq`, `tamer`        | Not there: more than 180 dB under          |
| `noise-floor`                | 75 dB under or more                        |
| `tape`                       | 35 to 52 dB under                          |
| `analog-delay` (Mix 0.35)    | 41 to 47 dB under                          |
| `hall-reverb`, Mix 0.2       | 17 to 23 dB under                          |
| `hall-reverb`, Mix 0.3       | 12 to 19 dB under                          |
| `hall-reverb`, Mix 0.4       | 7 to 15 dB under                           |
| `plate-reverb`, Mix 0.2      | 11 to 20 dB under                          |
| `plate-reverb`, Mix 0.3      | 6 to 13 dB under                           |
| `hall-reverb` alone (Mix 1)  | 2 to 14 dB over the ear's own              |
| `plate-reverb` alone (Mix 1) | Over the ear's own on five keys of the six |

- **`ambient-eq`, `tamer`, `noise-floor`, `tape` and `analog-delay` keep the
  sides apart.** After them the beat is still between the ears. The echoes of
  `analog-delay` taken alone (Mix 1, Spread 0, the key at 440 Hz) carry the
  other ear's tone 12 dB under.
- **The two reverbs differ in what they take in, and neither keeps a two-ear
  beat in its tail.** `plate-reverb` adds its two inputs to one before the
  plate: fed a sound that is all side (the left the reverse of the right) it
  puts out no plate at all. `hall-reverb` keeps its two sides apart on the
  way in: fed the same sound, its room alone is 6.8 dB more side than mid.
  But the room alone holds both tones in each ear, the other ear's 2 to 14 dB
  over the ear's own on the six keys, and so does the plate alone on five of
  the six. That is a beat in the air. What stays between the ears is the dry
  sound beside the tail, so Mix sets how much is left, as the table shows.
- **How to read the figures.** A tone 12 dB under ripples the ear's own by
  4.4 dB, which is what Ears 0.6 gives; 20 dB under is 1.7 dB.
- **What the bank does with this.** Where the point of a preset is the beat
  between the ears, its chain is made of effects that keep the sides apart;
  where a room follows, its description says that the room blends the ears.
- **A host can undo all of it.** A track panned off centre (the engine's pan
  moves part of one side into the other), a send to a reverb shared with
  other tracks, a width control turned down and a mono output each fold the
  ears together, and from there on the beat is in the air. No device can
  prevent that.

## Cautions

These are the research's own cautions. A caution is not a promise.

- **Keep it quiet.** A two-ear beat is heard at very low levels, and turning
  it up does not deepen it [[1]](#sources).
- **Stop if you feel dizzy or unwell.** Some studies measured worse mood after
  slow beats [[3]](#sources) [[25]](#sources).
- **Do not listen while driving**, or while working anything that needs your
  attention: listeners in one study dozed off [[6]](#sources).
- **With a history of seizures, ask a doctor first.** Reviewers advise it,
  though they found no report of harm [[35]](#sources).
- **Breathing at about 4.5 breaths a minute or slower can cause
  overbreathing** [[29]](#sources). If you feel light-headed, breathe more
  shallowly or stop. Two of Breath Pacer's counts are slower than that
  ("Fours all round" at 3.75 breaths a minute and "In 4 hold 7 out 8" at
  about 3.2), and Slow to, which lengthens a cycle to as much as twice,
  can take other counts there. No text recommends a pattern.
- Above about 15 pulses a second, hard edges and high Depth sound rough
  [[24]](#sources) and tire the ear, so keep Depth moderate.

## What measurement says is weak

Nobody has listened to any of the nine. Each has a row, written by its checker
from measurements, in the table
[The beat and breath devices: what measurement says is weak](../devices.md#the-beat-and-breath-devices-what-measurement-says-is-weak)
in devices.md. One line of each:

- **Binaural.** Unproven: that the beat between the ears is heard at all,
  since the listener makes it and only the two tones are measured. Listen
  first, on headphones, to one key of "Six a second" and lift one earpiece:
  the beat should stop.
- **Isochronic.** Weak: a held key gets louder as the others die, 6.0 dB with
  three of four keys let go. Listen first to a chord let go one key at a time
  at a short Release.
- **Coloured Noise.** Unproven: whether the swells feel unpaced and whether a
  band on white noise reads as a pitch; and at the Volume it starts at, the
  bank's held phrase reads -31.3 dBFS, 1.3 dB under the bank's floor. Listen
  first to High cut swept down from its open end on white noise at 44.1 kHz.
- **Breath Pacer.** Unproven: whether the swell is easy to follow and whether
  the marks sit well in the air; and a key played again within 1.67 times
  Release joins the breath where it stands, so it can land in a silent Rest.
  Listen first to the default on a held chord through two breaths.
- **Still Point.** Weak: where the sine meets a low key the tone stays down,
  3 dB under for up to 2.2 s on D2 with pure keys at Level 0.5 and the
  default Time and Reach, and at Level 1 it fades right out. Listen first to one low key held through a meeting at
  Level 0.5, then at Level 1 with Time 60.
- **Twin Bars.** Weak: a held key ducks when keys land beside it, 2.3 dB
  within a millisecond for three more keys, and comes back as they die;
  whether that is heard as pumping only an ear can say. Listen first, on
  headphones, to one low key held on "Four a second" while short chords are
  played above it.
- **Ear Shift.** Unproven: that two ears 6 Hz apart are heard as one sound
  beating inside the head; and a note near Low or High wavers at half of
  Beat, 4.4 dB half an octave from the edge. Listen first, on headphones, to a
  held chord round middle C at the defaults, each ear alone and then both.
- **Band Pulse.** Weak: the split turns phase and nothing trims the peak, so
  even at Depth 0 a bare square wave fed at -1 dBFS comes out 9.5 dB over its
  own peak. Listen first to a bare bright synth fed hot, for clipping after
  the device.
- **Winding Down.** Unproven: that the pulse is heard to slow, or to quicken,
  without end, and not as layers that come and go. Listen first to "Two
  crossing" over two whole halvings, for its swell of 1.2 dB.
