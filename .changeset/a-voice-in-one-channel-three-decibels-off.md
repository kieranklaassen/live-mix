A source in one channel played at three levels, by what its strip had been used for: as loud as it came through a strip with no nodes yet, 3.01 dB quieter each side once the strip's nodes were made (the first touch of a fader, a pan, a mute or an insert), and as loud as it came again with a compressor, a WASM device or anything else ahead of the pan that gives two channels out for one in. So a voice recorded in one channel was 3.01 dB louder with a compressor in its chain than without one. A StereoPanner has two laws, equal power for one channel and unity at the centre for two, and which it used went by what it was fed. The pan stage of a strip, of a rack's chain and of the utility now takes two channels whatever it is fed (`createPan`), so one channel plays as two alike channels do.

**Levels that change**, which is why this is a minor bump. A source in one channel is up to 3.01 dB louder at the centre than it was (0 dB each side, from -3.01 dB) wherever a pan stage was fed one channel: a strip whose nodes are made, a strip with no nodes that plays into a group (the group's own pan took the 3 dB), a rack's chain and the utility. Panned hard to one side it is 6.02 dB louder in that side, as two alike channels always were. Whatever is after the pan hears the same rise: a post-fader send, a meter's tap, and a group's or the master's compressor, limiter or ducker, whose gain reduction moves with it. What was fed two channels plays as it did: a source in two channels, a source through a compressor or a WASM device, one that shares its strip or its group with a source in two channels, and a strip with no nodes that plays straight to the master. No `AudioParam` event changes. `createPan` is exported for a host that makes a pan stage of its own.
