Device plates have a second look, `studio`: the plate as a photograph from straight above, lit by one soft lamp from the upper left. Satin paint with a rolled edge, knobs of turned aluminium anodised in the plate's own colour (one kind of knob for each of the skin's caps), the display and the preset cell under glass let into the box, the `+n` and `Edit` cells as low caps, the lamp in a bezel on a hex nut. `<PlateLookProvider look="studio">` asks for it over any part of the page and `look="studio"` on a `DevicePlate` for one plate; `usePlateLook`, `useStudioLook`, `studioReady` and `studioPalette` are exported with them. The flat plate is still the default, and nothing changes without one of the two.

The photograph is one canvas under the plate's own parts, drawn by one fragment shader in one WebGL context shared by every plate, from where the parts stand on the page: effects and instruments, upright, lying and spread, opened or not. Nothing a hand or a script finds changes with the look: the plate's size, every control with its test id and info text, where each knob, cell and lamp stands, and the display, which draws what it drew (it lays no ground of its own on the glass: `DisplayColours.ground`). It is drawn again only when a knob turns, the lamp goes on or off, a part moves or the skin's colours change. The colours are made from the skin's three, with the lettering held at 4.5 to 1 or better on the paint. Where the photograph cannot be taken (no WebGL, a lost context, more than 64 knobs, on the server) the plate is the flat one, with nothing of the studio look in its markup.
