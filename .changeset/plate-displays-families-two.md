Plate displays, the second batch, and the plate itself.

- Pitch: the Pitch Shifter and the Spectral Drifter draw a ruler in semitones with their voices or grains on it, the Octaves five bars to drag, the Half Speed the cycle it holds and where it is read, the Frequency Shifter a scale in hertz with the partials of a tone moved by the shift, and the Lattice its scale with the note played and where each voice is sent. The Octaves, Half Speed, Frequency Shifter and Lattice report readings for the display; their sound is unchanged.
- Texture: the Sustainer and the Pad Follower draw the last eight seconds of what is played and what they hold of it, the Cloud its grains over the sound it keeps, the Glitch its slices and what each does, the Blur the spectrum that hangs on with its tone, and the Cascade the score of its replays. All six report readings for the display; their sound is unchanged.
- Delay: the Delay, the Analog Delay, the Tape Echo and the Echo Memory draw the repeats of one loud click on a scale of seconds, over the sound that went in running on into its repeats. A point sets Time and Feedback. The Analog Delay, the Tape Echo and the Echo Memory report readings for the display; their sound is unchanged.
- Loops: the Tape Loop draws its tape between two decks, the Reverse Delay the chunks it plays backwards, the Grain Delay its grains over what it holds, and the Micro Looper what it remembers and the loop within it. Length or Time can be dragged. All four report a clock of their own and where their heads are; their sound is unchanged, and a level is worked out only when a display asks.
- Wear: the Tape, the Vinyl, the Patina, the Radio, the Low Bitrate, the Vintage Digital and the Noise Floor draw what the medium does to the sound over its spectrum, with what it adds in the accent: a tape's roll-off, hiss and wobble, a record's wear, ticks and warp, a radio's band and static, a codec's lost bands and packets, a converter's steps and aliases, a bed of noise following the playing. The roll-off, wear, tuning, loss, rate and level can be dragged. Six of them report readings for the display; their sound is unchanged.
- A plate with a strip is 280 wide, as wide as one with a window, so a chain of them is even: its face of four knobs shares that room and the strip under it is 224 wide. A preset's name has room at the foot beside the plate's own; five plates print a short name (`Ambient Comp`, `Ducker`, `Convolver`, `Drifter`, `Freq Shifter`).
- On a plate with a display, and on one with neither display nor picture, the tools an app gives it (`actions`, with remove) stand two abreast in the column at the right, under the cell that opens the rest, so a row of tools never lies over the last knob. A plate with only a picture keeps the row.
- The names under a plate's knobs are one size. A word too long for its column is set closer and then narrower, never smaller.
- A display stands still after eight seconds of silence in and out (`live.settle` for one that shows more of the past than that) and runs again at the first sound, a moved knob or a pointer. `settledDisplays()` says how many are standing.
- The plate bench gives each plate as many tools as an app does, and `window.plates.registry`.
