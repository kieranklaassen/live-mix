Ten more effects for ambient music, each built on one idea no other device in the library has: echoes placed like stars or like a skipping stone, loops that turn against each other or wear away through a room, a tail that melts, grains that fall, sparks, a cloud with no tail, a sound that walks away, and a round from one line. Each is a spec device on `cpp/kit` with a native test that measures what it claims, sixteen presets of its own, a description of every control, and a plate of its own colour with a display that shows what the device is doing and can be taken hold of. Each was built by one worker and checked by a second, who measured it again and put right what that found. None is an emulation of a product, and none has been listened to: every trait is held by a measurement, and `docs/devices.md` says for each one where a first listen should go.

Echoes and loops:

- `constellation` (Constellation): up to twelve echoes scattered over one span of time like stars, each with its own moment, side and brightness, in a pattern that never falls on a pulse.
- `skipping-stone` (Skipping Stone): echoes that land closer and closer together, softer and duller each time, crossing from side to side; or the other way, slowing down and spreading out.
- `orbits` (Orbits): two to five loops of unequal lengths record what you play and turn against each other, so the layers never meet the same way twice.
- `generations` (Generations): a loop that records itself again through a small room on every pass, until what you played has dissolved into the room's own ringing tones.

Tails and grains:

- `melt` (Melt): a tail that ages: the longer a sound has rung, the further it sags in pitch, darkens and blurs, or turned the other way lifts and evaporates.
- `falling` (Falling): small pieces of the sound that each fall or rise in pitch as they fade, from a rain of quick drops to slow sighs.
- `glints` (Glints): sparse bright sparks, short pieces of what is playing an octave or two up, thrown somewhere between the speakers.

Space:

- `fog` (Fog): blurs every attack into a soft cloud with no tail at all: the diffusion of a reverb without the reverb.
- `distance` (Distance): moves the sound away as air and a room do, quieter, darker, later and narrower, and lets it wander nearer and farther with a bend in its pitch.

A round:

- `canon` (Canon): up to four followers repeat what you play, each one gap later and at its own interval, forwards or backwards, and the last can feed the line again.
