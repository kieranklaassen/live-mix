# Isochronic: what is weak

Nobody has listened to this device; every figure here is a measurement. The figure that worries most comes from the family's level law: the sum is divided by the root of the keys' envelopes squared, so a held key gets louder as the others die. With three of four keys let go it rises 6.0 dB in all, 1.79 dB in the first 100 ms at Release 2 s and 2.3 dB at 0.5 s; with seven of eight let go, 9.0 dB in all and up to 3.9 dB in 100 ms. By the same law a chord of five let go together holds its level for 0.2 s at Release 1.5 s, and for 2 s at Release 15 s, before it falls. Next, Rate thrown at an Edge of 2 ms while a key sounds: the gate catches up along a straight ramp, which puts 24 dB under the tone above 1.5 kHz, 21 to 24 dB more than the pulse's own edge, with no step; whether that is heard as a tick is not known. Next, with Air part way eight keys at full strength pass the clip's knee: a chord on 23 samples in ten minutes, eight keys on one pitch on 1.9 % of samples, and the clip holds them. Tone alone and noise alone stay under it. The noise is the same in both ears and 3 dB under the tone. A change of Duty or Edge waits for the next pulse, up to 2.4 s at the slowest Rate. Listen first to a chord let go one key at a time at a short Release, then to Rate turned quickly at Edge 2 ms, then to a full chord with Air near a quarter.
