Wheel and Dog: nobody has heard it, every claim is a figure. The figures that worry most are the bark's. It is made hot and held under a ceiling, so from a push a little over the dog's threshold upward it is one loudness: with Dog at 0.55 the loudest 20 ms are 4 dB under the drones' level at a push of 0.3, level with them at 0.4, and 1 dB over at 1; at Dog 1 and Depth 1 they are 3 dB over the drones' average and 1 dB under the drones in the same push. That is inside the rule it was built to, but all of the bark lies in 2 to 6 kHz, where the strings are 12 dB down, so to an ear it may well stand far in front, and with Third drone turned up it goes up with it. Whether it is a wooden buzz or an electric one, and whether its three fixed resonances are heard as a vowel, only an ear can say. The strings are a drawn wave with a filter after it, not a bowed string: the bow's pressure and speed change its level, its edge and its hiss, and nothing chaotic ever happens at an attack. Cotton is 9 % hiss by energy against 0.35 % at the defaults, which may not be whisper enough.

Also unproven by ear: at the default Detune the third drone stands within a twentieth of a cent of a pure fifth, so over minutes its fundamental and the low drone's third partial turn slowly against each other and the level moves 2.6 dB in five minutes (0.7 dB in sixteen seconds); a melody key on A over D drones beats and at some moments loses 6 dB of its fundamental, as two such strings do, while a key on a drone's very own note leans on it by up to six cents for up to a second and then stands at one level; a D an octave up has no such help and moves 1.8 dB with the moment it is struck. In Duo a single key has both strings, so it falls 4.8 dB when a second key takes one of them. With Rosin and Brightness at 1 the folded partials of a key from C6 up are 45 to 50 dB under it, at the defaults 60 to 80. Six presets come within 1 dB of the clip's knee at the hardest touch on every key ("Long run down" 0.1 dB, "Bright reel" 0.4, "Mad dog", "Raw wheel" and "Wooden keys" 0.5, "Four to the turn" 1.0) and none passes it on the keys tried, drones on D. "Turning wheel" and "Three barks a turn" are the nearest two presets, 1.3 dB apart on the print. Listen first to "Three barks a turn" on a held D2, for the bark against the drones; then play D3, A3 and D4 over it, for how the melody sits in the drones; then let a chord go on "Long run down".
