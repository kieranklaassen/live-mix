**Ear Shift.** Unproven: that two ears 6 Hz apart are heard as one sound beating inside the head. Measured is only that 440 Hz in reads 443 Hz in the left ear and 437 Hz in the right at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, natively and through the built module, with the other sideband 44 dB under or better across the band. The figure that worries most is the waver beside a band edge: a note on Low or High is half shifted and half not, so each ear hears it fall to silence at half of Beat; half an octave from the edge it still wavers 4.4 dB, an octave away 1.1 dB. Low starts at 60 Hz for that reason: notes from A2 to A4 then waver 1.5 dB at the most (28 dB at 147 Hz with Low at 150), but bass notes from 42 to 85 Hz waver 4.4 dB or more, and partials near High always do (880 Hz reads 12 dB at the defaults). Also measured and not judged: the mono fold at Ears 1 pulses to silence at Beat, which is what speakers or a reverb fed from both sides make of it; peaks rise by the turn of phase, 7.4 dB on white noise and 8.2 dB on a square wave at an unchanged level, though on the piano phrase fed 1 dB under full scale no preset passed full scale from twenty starts (worst 0.4 dB over the input); at 192 kHz the kit's phase pair runs out and a 60 Hz tone keeps its other sideband only 24 dB under; a knob thrown on a soft low tone leaves a tick 50 dB under the tone. Listen first on headphones to a held chord round middle C at the defaults, each ear alone and then both; then to a bass note near 60 Hz; then to the same folded to mono.
