Still Point: nobody has listened to it. Unproven: how the sine's slide sounds when a far key is added (a key two octaves up takes the sine up to two octaves in about two seconds, through pitches that are neither key), and whether a chord let go slowly behaves as a player expects (the sweep's range is kept only when the fingers lift within tens of milliseconds: a chord 27 semitones wide keeps 14 with its keys let go 300 ms apart). The figure that worries most is how long the tone stays down where the sine meets a low key. With pure keys at Level 0.5 the tone dips 6 dB and sits 3 dB under for up to 2.2 s on D2 at the default Time and Reach, and up to 16 s at Time 120 with Reach 0.1. At Level 1 it fades right out and is 12 dB under for up to 1.2 s at the default Time and Reach, and up to 9.2 s at Time 120 with Reach 0.1. The preset Deep slow beat was lowered to Level 0.75 so that it never reaches 12 dB under, yet it still sits 6 dB under for up to 7.4 s on A1. Listen first to one low key held through a meeting at Level 0.5 and then at Level 1 with Time 60, next to a key added two octaves up in mid sweep, and last to Split and pure on speakers, where the two ears fold into one beat that can cancel.
