Twin Bars: nobody has heard it, every claim is a figure. The figure that worries most is the room law that keeps sixteen voices under the clip. It turns the whole sum down as keys are added, so a held key at gain 0.8 falls 2.3 dB within a millisecond when three more keys land beside it (5.1 dB at full strength) and comes back at 2 to 4 dB per 100 ms as they die, faster with Damp turned up. Above 1 kHz the fall is 52 to 73 dB under the strokes that cause it, so it is not a click, but whether it is heard as pumping only an ear can say, and inside a big chord a key rings longer at first than Decay says. Also unproven by ear: with the hardest mallet and the brightest Tone four wooden keys at gain 0.8 pass the clip's knee (peak 0.68, eight metal keys 0.59), though no preset as shipped does on eight keys; a roll below full strength swells 3.5 to 4.3 dB over one stroke where a roll at full strength is held to 1.3 dB; a key struck again keeps its beat only while its low tone is less than 40 dB under the new stroke, after which the beat starts again at its crest; a key rolled faster than Scatter strikes its second bar less often than its first. Listen first, on headphones, to one low key held on Four a second while short four note chords are played above it, for the held key ducking and coming back; then strike one key every half second on Slow metal bowl, for the beat running on through each stroke.
