Silent Keys: nobody has heard it, every claim is a figure. The figure that worries most is how tonal the answer stays when a rhythm is put through it: at the default patch the strings alone come out 1.7 dB under a made drum loop, 2.7 under made rain and made speech, 10.5 under white noise and 10.9 under 5 ms clicks, but a loop is answered mostly by the low drum gliding past the lowest keys, and a chord away from it falls (the list's seventh chord, rooted at E4, is 18.6 dB under the same loop), and a hat with nothing under 7 kHz wakes next to nothing (70 dB under itself), the strings having no partial up there. So whether the rhythm is heard as the chord's own, or as a chord under a loop, only an ear can say. The default Lift was raised from 18 to 22 dB and the default Sharpness lowered from 0.55 to 0.5 (with a gate that now bites only in the upper half of Sharpness) to make noise speak; the price is the contrast between notes. A low D into C3 E3 G3 wakes its two shared partials about 15 and 20 dB under the C3 a low C wakes, and the whole answer is 11 dB under, where it was 18; E3 stays 60 dB and more under C3, so the staccato example holds with room. Raised to 36 dB, Lift brings the two answers closer still.

Also unproven by ear: the level of what rings depends on the loudness of the last second and not only on what wakes it. A tone held on a partial rises 7 dB over a third of a second when bursts of noise begin above it and then stays level under them, and the first quiet notes after a loud passage are answered as if they were louder. A steady tone near a partial at a part-way Mix stands against the string's own phase: it came out between 3.2 dB under its dry share and 1.6 dB over the input. Bass at full adds about 5 dB to a phrase whose notes are in the chord and all of it stands in the centre. A sound whose channels are a quarter turn apart is heard by every second partial only: a rich one loses 2.3 dB at the most, a lone sine on one partial can be missed (43 dB under in the worst case found). White noise up to the top of the band is answered 3 dB weaker at 192 kHz and 1 dB at 22 kHz, since the gate measures a share of all there is. At Ring 30 s with Every a quarter of a second, noise is answered 3 dB weaker than a chord that stays. A walk starts again after the strings have died and two seconds or one Every of exact silence; a source with a noise floor never gives it that silence, so the walk then runs on, and at a middling Ring (2 s, sounds 3 s apart) whether it starts again depends on whether the strings had died. Listen first, on headphones, to short brushed noise and speech on the default patch with Mix at 1, for whether the chord speaks with the syllables; then to one bass note every two seconds on Walk of four, for the change of chord under a ringing tail; then to Glass strings on anything with clicks in it, the brightest preset.
