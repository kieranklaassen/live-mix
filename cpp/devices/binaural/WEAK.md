Binaural. Nobody has heard it. The signal is proven (each ear's tone sits half of Beat from the key to five decimals at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, the browser build matches the native one to the sample), but the beat between the ears is made by the listener and no measurement here shows that it is heard, nor that the 5 ms fade of a stolen voice or a jump of Beat under a held chord passes unnoticed. The figure that worries most is the bed: it is added on top of the chord scale, so with Bed at 1 the peaks pass the soft clip's knee (0.15 % of samples with one key at full gain, 0.31 % with eight, peaks to 0.80), where the clip adds tones of its own inside each ear; no factory preset reaches the knee (worst peak 7.4 dB under full scale) but a hard touch with Bed up will. Cross is 3.0 dB lower in level than Pair and Warmth at 1 is 3.7 dB lower than Warmth at 0 at the same peak, so two of the three Cross presets sit 3 dB under the middle of the bank. Listen first, on headphones, to one key of "Six a second" and lift one earpiece: the beat should stop. Then "Forty crossed" against "Forty in the air", then hold eight keys and strike a ninth, then play A1 over A2 (two keys on one pitch, which cancelled by up to 54 dB before the fix), then "Dark bed, three" at a hard touch for grit.
