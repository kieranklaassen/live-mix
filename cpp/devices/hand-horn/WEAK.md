Hand Horn: nobody has heard it, every claim is a figure. The figure that worries most is that the shadow is judged by centroid and level alone. On the C crook at the default Shadow, F4 (51 cents) has its centroid at 585 Hz and 2.1 dB less level than open, A4 (69) 557 Hz and 3.4 dB, F sharp 4 (102) 524 Hz and 5.5 dB, against 689 Hz for an open G4; weighed by amplitude instead of power F4 and A4 are within 2 Hz of each other (716 and 717), and only against their own open tones does A4 come out darker (x 0.73 against 0.79). E flat 4 (86 cents) is 11 Hz under F sharp 4, since it is three semitones lower and the rule knows only cents. The nasal band is a share of what lies above 1 kHz: over what is above it, it rises by 9.5, 9.7 and 16.1 dB, but of the whole sound it has less with the hand in than without, and only at a full stop does it stand over the harmonics below it, by 2 to 3 dB. Whether that says "stopped" or only "dull" an ear must say. A stopped key under C3 peaks about 5 dB under an open one (minus 26.4 to minus 28.7 dBFS at gain 0.7 against about minus 22), so the low half of a chord on one crook is uneven by design; the level guide's floor is minus 24. Raising it meets the clip: six keys at full gain peak at 0.47 of the 0.5 the knee allows, worst among about 300 chords that were tried, at the default patch.

Also unproven by ear: the lip is a switch with a dead range. On the C crook in equal temperament the keys from C3 to C5 open at 2, 4, 12, 14, 31, 41, 49, 51 and 60 cents, so nothing changes between Lip 14 and 31 or between 32 and 40: the rule says so and the description says that a small turn may change nothing, but a player who turns it there hears nothing. At Lip 52 and over F4 itself opens, and the covered keys the device is about go with it. A lipped up note is thinner by a fundamental up to half gone and 1.5 dB, at most, which at B4 is 0.9 dB. Tremble lowers an open note and then raises it by the same 28 cents though an open hand has no further to open. Two ids on one key are one horn, the newer: the older goes out in 30 ms, so a long Attack leaves a hole of seconds under a held key. A key struck again dips about 7 dB for 20 ms, the tongue or a fault. Every own preset keeps six keys at full gain under the knee on a few dozen chords (the nearest are "Air in the tube" at 0.48 and "Dark corner" at 0.48), and the bank's sounds were not made when this was measured. Listen first, on headphones, to a C major scale and then an F major scale on "Hand on the C crook" in an open octave, for whether F and A turn dark or merely quiet; then to a slide of a tenth on "Ladder of a tenth" for whether the rungs are heard as rungs; then to "Rough start" for grit that may be too fast to hear as a crack.
